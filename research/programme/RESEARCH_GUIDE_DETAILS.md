## Current research questions

- **Catastrophe-stop position data:** Previously, synthetic `unrealized_pl=NaN` or `cost_basis=+inf` produced no stop, while `unrealized_pl=−inf` produced a stop. The undeployed change blocks buys and sweeps when the enabled stop cannot assess a held position, identifies the affected symbols, and continues signal exits and assessable catastrophe stops. Pending orders are not canceled. An independent synthetic review found no blocking issue in that safeguard; broker behavior remains unverified. Subsequent local changes save non-finite position values as JSON null, treat missing quantities as unresolved split evidence, and report snapshot write failures in the run result with a notification attempt. A later independent synthetic review found no blocking issue in the scoped P&L and snapshot failure handling; alert delivery and actual broker payloads remain unverified. All changes remain undeployed.
- **Invalid threshold alert path:** The undeployed trader now attempts a halt notification and raises before acquiring its run lock or submitting orders. Its top-level CLI entry point can read the broker account first. The [paper wrapper](../../scripts/paper_trade.sh) also has a nonzero-exit alert trap; actual alert delivery has not been checked. This is distinct from validating the positions used by the stop.

**Owner direction:** Retire new GO/NO-GO determinations. Report the evidence, whether any preset thresholds were met, uncertainty, and separately any actual action decision. Preserve historical records as history.

- **Finding stocks:** The [low-accruals screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-02-accruals-feasibility.md#first-screen-result--2026-11-10) did not meet its proposed hurdles; an independent reviewer reproduced the result. The separate [52-week-high screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-10-high52-screen-results.md) also failed both fixed hurdles. The [exploratory R&D screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-10-rd-exploratory-screen.md) had high older returns but a deep recent loss and weak statistical evidence. The [earnings-consistency screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-10-earnings-consistency-screen.md) showed no reliable winner. All four were independently recomputed; none identifies a validated stock picker. The owner-approved [monthly reversal screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-11-short-term-reversal-results.md) failed both specified hurdles; an independent reviewer reproduced the result. The [low-variance screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-11-low-variance-results.md) also failed both return hurdles; an independent reviewer reproduced the figures. Its shallower historical drawdown is descriptive, not an approved protection verdict. The [accounting-score screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-11-accounting-score-results.md) failed both return hurdles despite a strong full-period return; its historical drawdown was deep. Its results were independently reproduced. The [expected-investment-growth screen](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-11-expected-growth-results.md) passed its recent-return hurdle but missed its full-history statistical hurdle; an independent reviewer reproduced the figures. The [feasibility inventory](../stock-signals/feasibility/2026-11-02-free-data-stock-feasibility.md) records the data and prior-study limitations.
- **Managing losses:** [Three ways to study protection](verdict-framework/2026-11-10-protection-measures-explained.md) explains deepest fall, a bad year and recovery time. The owner indicated a 1–5 year investment horizon and chose to explore all three outcomes; the exact study horizon, comparator and acceptable cost of protection remain open.
- **Time-exit research limitation:** The engine's optional `td_stop` schedules exits from entry signals rather than filled positions. It can close a later trade early, skip an entry or hold a trade past its limit; [expected-failure tests](../../tests/engine/test_time_stop_entry_reuse.py) reproduce these cases. The engine warns when this option is used and rejects it with intraday stops; neither fixes the timing. Old-engine time-stop studies, including the exit-rule sweep, used the same signal-shift logic and did not warn. Their impact needs review; no historical result has been recalculated or reclassified. The default ensemble does not set a time stop.
- **What this means for the exit sweep:** Its [design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-28-exit-rule-sweep-design.md) mixed time-stop and no-time-stop variants in one walk-forward selection. The timing defect directly concerns the time-stop variants; the baseline and take-profit-only variants do not use that timer, but selection among all variants could still be affected. The [audit](../audits/root-cause-2026-11-04/lenses/2026-11-04-lens2-verdict-audit.md) found no raw artifact for the sweep, so the size or direction of the impact is unknown. The historical NO-GO remains recorded, and it does not establish that correctly timed exits cannot help.
- **Intraday stop review:** The opt-in path applies configured slippage to simulated stop and gap exits. By default the initial stop uses the entry bar's close; researchers can choose `stop_entry_price="fillprice"` in the simulation's base configuration to use the simulated entry fill instead. With a $100 close, 1% entry slippage and a 10% stop, those choices start the trigger at $90 and $90.90 respectively. Walk-forward diagnostics record the choice when a price stop is active. The simulator tests a bar against the prior trailing stop before raising it from that bar's open or high; an actual trailing order may behave differently. A missing bar while a position is held fails the run, with the affected date and column, instead of assuming a sale before a halt or delisting. A prospective delisting policy and real execution validation are still needed. Open, high and low inputs are expanded and validated per simulation chunk; close and signal grids remain allocated outside the chunk estimate.

  - **Frozen-tape input check:** The engine's present OHLC checks reject six rows in the 1,110,874-row frozen tape: one nonpositive open and five inconsistent high/low bars. Four ordering errors share May 5, 2021; discrepancies are 0.12%–0.92%, not floating-point noise. The [audit](_intraday_stop_ohlc_check_20261111.json) and [fixture join](_intraday_stop_ohlc_membership_20261111.json) show that the fixed 2021–April 2026 replay universe includes the five in-window labels; loading its May 5 bars and validating with no entries still fails. This does not establish that any strategy held them, why the bars disagree, or how to correct them. No trades or returns were calculated.
  - **Second-file comparison:** On those dates the frozen comparison file has internally consistent bars for AVB, CTRA and WBD, inconsistent bars for HUBB and UA, and no BK row. HUBB and UA are Yahoo-labelled in that file; AVB and CTRA are Tiingo-labelled. A [separate indexed UA quote](_intraday_stop_ua_quote_check_20261111.json) reports a lower low than the frozen files, illustrating why clipping the low to the open is not a verified repair. Source lineage, price basis and security identity are not established, so none is an automatic replacement. See the [comparison](_intraday_stop_ohlc_comparison_20261111.json).
  - **Halt and carried bars:** [Nasdaq reported](https://ir.nasdaq.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nasdaq-halts-svb-financial-group) halting SIVB before the open on March 10, 2023; a later [company filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312523073665/d485308d8k.htm) reports the halt and scheduled March 28 suspension. The frozen tape has several flat bars equal to the March 9 close in that interval, including four with positive volume; the [spot check](_sivb_halt_bar_check_20261111.json) does not independently establish every day's trading status. A [synthetic test](../../tests/engine/test_intraday_stop_inputs.py) shows that the engine can simulate an exit on a flat bar when no halt mask is supplied. Price presence or positive volume alone does not establish an executable exit. Do not use a retrospective list to remove securities from a universe.
  - **Optional research guard:** Signal simulations can accept a caller-supplied boolean `halt_mask` and reject a position overlapping any flagged bar, including positions still open at the end. Walk-forward diagnostics record whether a mask was supplied and a SHA-256 of its ordered dates, labels and boolean values; a study must pin the source and its provenance separately. This is a conservative rejection after simulation, not a realistic resumption-fill model; it relies on independently verified, complete halt histories and does not certify a run when the mask is absent or incomplete. It has synthetic coverage only and is not deployed.
  - **Interval conversion:** The experimental [halt helper](../intraday/_lib/halt_intervals.py) flags supplied sessions touched by supplied halt intervals. It uses the supplied session open and close, flags a halt at the close and carries an unresolved halt through the supplied calendar. A resumption exactly at the open clears that session. These conservative rules do not verify source completeness, publication times or actual execution; retrospective flags are for auditing, not hindsight-based security selection.
- **Cash and fills:** With shared cash, the default simulator processes symbols in column order. In intraday mode, automatic sequencing lets a synthetic stop sale fund a close-time entry in either order; it also reorders ordinary signal exits and entries, even when no stop fires, and can change allocations when position sizes differ. The research API accepts `order_sequence="auto"` or `"default"` and `stop_exit_price="stopmarket"` or `"stoplimit"` for either mode; diagnostics record the effective fill rule only when a price stop is active. In this simulator `stopmarket` applies configured slippage to stop exits, while `stoplimit` does not; neither models a limit order that fails to fill. Defaults differ by mode, so a comparison must explicitly match both choices. Stop trigger timing and reference prices still differ. Automatic sequencing with percent sizing is approximate, and competing buys can still depend on column order when cash is scarce. Stop-market slippage can produce a simulated fill below the day's low, and it also applies to take-profit exits; that is a market-exit assumption, not a limit-order fill model. These limitations need a study-specific treatment before comparing exit rules.
- **Price history for stops:** The [tape audit](../audits/root-cause-2026-11-04/lenses/2026-11-04-lens1-backtest-validity.md) found adjustment seams between fetches: prices can jump because old and new bars use different dividend or split adjustment vintages. Even if each day's open, high, low and close agree with one another, a seam between days could trigger a false stop. The loader's use of adjusted prices does not establish that a particular frozen tape is seam-free; any exit study must address this before relying on results.
- **Frozen-tape spot check:** For `VZ`, the 2026-11-06 tape (SHA-256 `6573e840…789b2e`) divided by the separate frozen adjusted-price file (SHA-256 `7b58eeaf…c5f3f5`) was about 1.016999 on 2026-07-14 through 07-17 and 1.001464 on July 20–21. This is consistent with the previously identified adjustment seam in the specific tape, not a verified correction or an independent source check. No strategy returns were calculated.
- **Broader adjustment check:** A [reproducible diagnostic](../stock-signals/feasibility/code/price_seam_audit.py) compared exact ticker labels across those frozen files from January 2021 through October 2026. Of 828,910 consecutive matched-session pairs across 592 labels, 71 pairs across 59 labels had a relative price-ratio shift of at least 1%; the [result](../stock-signals/feasibility/_price_seam_audit_20261110.json) records every flag and the hashes. Forty flags fall on July 20, 2026, the date of the previously identified VZ seam; all 40 ratios fell, with a median relative shift of −1.15%. An independent reviewer reproduced those numbers and, in a post-hoc check at a 0.2% threshold, found 221 of 558 matched labels shifted at least that much on July 20. The original 1% cutoff therefore misses smaller changes; it also misses gradual drift, constant offsets and gaps between matching sessions. Neither threshold measures the number of errors. This clustering does not establish a common cause for every label. Shared upstream data, ticker identity, corporate actions and missing sessions limit the comparison; flagged pairs are not necessarily errors, and unflagged pairs are not certified clean.
- **Different faults in the same cluster:** `AVB` falls from 192.53 to 68.69 in the frozen tape across July 17–20, while the comparison file records 192.53 and 191.84. The tape's `EQR` close on July 20 is 68.80, consistent with the earlier audit's finding that `AVB` tracks `EQR` from that date. `CTVA` falls from 87.11 to 12.99 in the tape while the comparison's adjusted close stays near 13.5. Corteva completed a [one-for-one Vylor share distribution on October 1, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2128626/000119312526414335/d92652d8k.htm). The comparison file records a `stock_splits` factor of 6.665 that day; its July 17 close of 13.098275 multiplied by 6.665 equals 87.30, matching a [published historical quote](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/quote/CTVA/history/) for that day. This supports a change of price basis around the later spin-off as the explanation for the tape break, though the provider's precise adjustment method and any usable repair remain unverified. Neither case establishes a common cause for the other flags.
- **Effect on the fixed replay window:** The July 20 cluster falls after the 2021-01-31 to 2026-04-30 replay window. A date filter of the same 1% flags yields 19 within that window and 52 outside it. A [revised join](../stock-signals/feasibility/_seam_membership_20261110_v2.json) to the vintage-1 membership fixture finds 15 exact ticker matches across 14 labels and four without an exact match. All four use `COR` in 2021; the fixture lists `ABC`, the company's former ticker, before its [2023 rename](https://investor.cencora.com/news/news-details/2023/AmerisourceBergen-becomes-Cencora-in-alignment-with-the-companys-growing-global-footprint-and-central-role-in-pharmaceutical-access-and-care/default.aspx). Thus the four unmatched rows do not establish nonmembership. The first [result](../stock-signals/feasibility/_seam_membership_20261110.json) is preserved along with its [original code](../stock-signals/feasibility/seam_membership_20261110_v1.py.txt); the revision normalizes both ticker formats and records snapshot age but does not resolve historical aliases. This retrospective list must not be used to exclude stocks using future information. The largest in-window flag is `APTV` on April 1, 2026, when the relative price ratio fell about 13%. The tape has closes of $69.44 on March 31 and $62.09 on April 1; the comparison file has the same unadjusted closes but an adjusted March 31 close of $60.41. Aptiv [distributed one Versigent share for every three Aptiv shares](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1521332/000152133226000037/aptv-20260401.htm) that day, so a research replay must account for the distribution rather than assume the parent-stock price alone is the whole holding. Neither the comparison file's adjustment method nor its value for the distribution has been validated. The fixture, ticker mapping and whether any strategy actually held these names remain unverified; neither the join nor the flags measure a strategy's performance.
- **Spin-off data gap:** Neither of the two frozen price files contains `VGNT` rows, although [Versigent began trading on April 1](https://ir.versigent.com/news/news-details/2026/Versigent-Launches-as-New-Publicly-Traded-Company/default.aspx) and joined the S&P SmallCap 600 on [April 2](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/indexnews/announcements/20260330-1482683/1482683_5aptv.pdf). A Yahoo request returned bars for all 21 April NYSE sessions, with finite, positive and internally consistent OHLC; the April 1 close was $27.85, also shown by [ChartExchange](https://chartexchange.com/symbol/nyse-vgnt/historical/). A later retrieval was frozen locally outside this repository with its query and receipt at `/home/flynn/ggTrader-refs/versigent-20261110/`; its CSV SHA-256 is `6af56982b414bd9f1f44a1b2800c2525afa98a460719ef9a0e766563efeb3bd8`. The sources may share upstream data. For illustration only, one Aptiv share at $62.09 plus one third of a Versigent share at $27.85 is $71.37 at that close; the previous Aptiv close was $69.44. A replay needs independent source and identity verification, entitlement and fractional-share rules; these close marks do not establish realizable fills or a strategy result.
- **Entitlement and fractional shares:** The [Versigent information statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2078008/000119312526104279/d65007dex991.htm) says fractional interests were pooled and sold, with net cash proceeds paid to holders later; it expected that process to take about two weeks. It also says a regular-way sale of Aptiv after the record date but before distribution transferred the right to the Versigent shares. The $71.37 figure above is therefore a mark of the combined entitlement, not the cash or fractional shares actually received by a holder of one Aptiv share. The actual proceeds, timing and individual tax basis are not established by the daily closes.
- **Yahoo sample use:** The [yfinance project](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance) says it is not endorsed by Yahoo, describes Yahoo's API as intended for personal use, and refers users to Yahoo for data rights. On this check Yahoo's terms page returned HTTP 999, so its current text and the rights to this sample were not verified. The local sample is not a license to redistribute raw data.
- **Intraday stop limitation:** By default, the lab signal wrapper does not pass open, high or low prices to the simulator for stop execution; highs and lows supplied for an ATR stop only set its distance. An explicit research-only `stop_open` input now opts into stop execution using matching OHLC bars; fully missing bars are allowed while no position is held, but an entry requested on a missing bar or any simulated holding spanning a missing bar makes the run fail before results are returned; partial bars are rejected; no existing caller opts in, and ATR stops are refused in this mode pending a timing review. Study code can opt in through `simulate_signal_out` or `run_wfo(..., intraday_stops=True)`; WFO records the mode and effective trailing, profit-target, ATR and time-stop settings in its diagnostics. The CLI does not expose it. In a synthetic test, a 10% trailing stop did not exit when the low fell 20% and the close recovered to the entry price. The [expected-failure example](../../tests/engine/test_time_stop_position_prototype.py) documents this close-based behavior. Separate synthetic tests show that the underlying Rust simulator can use OHLC to trigger on an intraday low and fill a gap at its open, and the new opt-in path passes those bars through. In synthetic examples, a prior bar's high raises the next bar's trailing stop, and a bar touching both a stop and profit target exits at the stop. On a synthetic six-stock panel, the intraday mode also matched simulations truncated at three earlier dates, including the selection metrics. Daily bars cannot establish which threshold was hit first. Open, high, low and close must also use a consistent corporate-action adjustment basis; the numerical bar checks cannot establish this. These examples do not establish real fills or repair the separate position-duration problem. Past studies have not been recomputed or reclassified.
- **Halt-data lead:** [NYSE](https://beta.nyse.com/trade/trading-halts) describes one year of historical news and LULD halts on its page. [Nasdaq's feed documentation](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/snippets/tradehaltaccordion.html) says a multi-day halt appears under its first halt date, not every affected day. A future check must therefore carry a halt through its actual resumption, handle unresolved halts and verify identity, coverage, timing and rights. Nasdaq calls its RSS feed free and requests no more than one query per minute; its linked [terms](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/content/administrationsupport/agreementstrading/THRSSFeedTermsCond.pdf) say accessing the feed accepts them and restrict modification. Their application to a research join remains unresolved. A [synthetic example](../../tests/engine/test_intraday_stop_inputs.py) shows that an intraday halt and reopening gap can share the same daily bar as continuous trading: the simulator fills a $90 stop at $90 in both, even when the example's first post-halt price is $75. That price is not a guaranteed fill. A historical-feed request failed in the web tool; no halt records were obtained or joined to a study.
- **Possible repair:** A [synthetic position-aware prototype](../../tests/engine/test_time_stop_position_prototype.py) handles those three examples using vectorbt's Numba callback path. Independent review found that a partially filled price-stop exit delayed its timer; the prototype now passes a test of that case by retaining the requested entry bar. It also passes limited examples with shared cash, a rejected entry and repeated signals. A seeded 250-bar, eight-stock comparison matched a separate simple reference for three holding limits, assuming constant prices and filled orders. Scope is long-only with no position accumulation or same-bar re-entry; an accumulation case remains an expected failure. In installed vectorbt 1.2.0, a custom callback selects Numba even if `engine="rust"` is requested. Thus this cannot be integrated into the project's Rust-only engine without a different implementation. These examples do not validate all fills, stop interactions or larger portfolios.
- **Rust interface:** The pinned static simulator has no `td_stop` argument, as checked by the prototype suite. The [vectorbt-rust 1.2.0 source distribution](https://files.pythonhosted.org/packages/0a/1d/6d5fc36ffc718fd57a61139336f4bb6d0f4f80ec64550080b0232dc4d8ca/vectorbt_rust-1.2.0.tar.gz), SHA-256 `9adc3b3cad70f7428fb4714ddbfc64d34aa06c8626938b032e98d1b5733e56c5` (matching `uv.lock`), tracks filled-position state for price stops internally; that is not a public duration-stop feature. An engine-level extension or a different validated implementation is needed.
- **Chunk-continuation constraint:** Inspection of the installed `vectorbt_rust.portfolio.simulate_from_signals_rs` signature found `init_cash` but no initial position, entry-time or stop-state argument. Starting a new signal simulation with ending cash alone cannot be assumed to carry an open holding or its stop state. The module separately exposes `ExecuteOrderState` and `execute_order_rs`; their existence does not establish an equivalent streaming signal simulator, including shared cash, stop priority and order sequencing. This is signature inspection, not a behavior test or proof that no other continuation route exists. The [inspection record](_rust_signal_continuation_api_20261111.json) captures the relevant signatures.
- **Synthetic chunk check:** A four-bar Rust simulation bought one share at $100 and sold it at $110, ending at $10,010. Splitting immediately after the purchase and passing only the first segment's $9,900 cash to a new simulation produced no sale and ended at $9,900: the open share was not carried over. The [input and output record](_rust_cash_only_chunk_probe_20261111.json) describes this invented example. This verifies the failure of cash-only continuation for one simple case, not every possible continuation technique or stop configuration.
- **Time exit and price stops:** In synthetic Numba prototype cases, a rejected exit at the three-bar limit was retried and filled on the following bar; a gap through a price stop on the bar the time exit was due instead filled at the opening price under the test's zero-slippage assumption. These are not broker or Rust-engine tests, and the time limit does not guarantee an exit when an order cannot fill.
- **Weight-simulation alignment:** The weight-based walk-forward scorer previously assumed every parameter group listed stocks in the same order. It now rejects inconsistent blocks before labels are converted to arrays. The three current built-in weight strategies all use the shared sorted-column constructor, including in the engine-rebuild commit `27fff069`; this reduces concern for those specific code paths, but does not prove every saved result or older engine path. The September cross-asset report predates this scorer, and the October French momentum screen used published portfolio returns. No prior verdict has been changed.
- **Understanding the previous stock sleeve:** The [replica artifact inventory](benchmark-objectives/2026-11-10-replica-attribution-artifact-inventory.md) explains what can be reconstructed from saved results and what is missing. It is a feasibility inventory, not an attribution result.
- **The cross-asset shadow:** The [decision brief](verdict-framework/2026-11-10-cross-asset-shadow-decision-brief.md) separates the historical shadow-only GO from unresolved shadow rules and observation records. It does not start a shadow or change the production freeze.
- **Choosing the next study:** The [objective decision brief](verdict-framework/2026-11-10-next-research-objective-brief.md) brings the open choices together. It is a proposal; no return test is authorized by the brief.
