**Fixed before this calculation; exploratory, not an independently preregistered test.** Compare the published `RD` portfolio `05`, the highest R&D expense relative to market equity, with French broad-market total return. This question is economically related to value: a low share price can increase the ratio. Preserve the earlier value-factor NO-GO and do not treat this as an independent replication.

Use the Open Source Asset Pricing 2026.10 original-construction portfolio file (SHA-256 `76599d94beab94cc8973d8a126e8efbed98ee79f677738c55b2389f17387ba96`) and the previously frozen French market-factor ZIP (SHA-256 `b03d39e4bb253dda0f14893db3b78e64b40b8d6549a0fc0fd4111dae84c98618`). Compare July 1975–December 2025 and January 1994–December 2025. July 1975 is within the paper's original sample and annual June formation convention; the end is the data release's documented end. Apply 1% annual cost as a monthly subtraction to the candidate, plus a 2% stress; market has no corresponding haircut. Compute 12-lag Newey–West t and CAGR difference, presenting the existing t ≥ 3 / +1 percentage point since-1994 standard as context, not a confirmed pass. ## Exploratory result — independently reviewed

The fixed calculation ([JSON](_rd_screen_20261110.json)) reports **18.20%** annualized versus **12.01%** for the broad market in July 1975–December 2025, after the candidate's assumed 1% annual cost. Its paired Newey–West t is **2.000** (one-sided p **0.0228**), below the existing t ≥ 3 threshold. Since 1994 it reports **14.05%** versus **10.81%**, a **+3.24 percentage-point/year** difference under the same assumption. The arithmetic comparison exceeds the existing +1-point hurdle, but the full-period t does not; the previously inspected source and multiple studies also prevent a confirmatory claim.

The portfolio's worst month-end drawdown was **−79.32%**, versus **−50.31%** for the market. This does not include intramonth lows, taxes, realized transaction costs or implementation on individual stocks. The 1% annual cost is unmeasured and may understate costs, particularly for an equal-weighted portfolio containing small companies. With a 2% annual cost assumption the since-1994 difference is +2.12 points/year. The since-1994 t is **1.13** (p **0.13**); the full-period t with 2% cost is **1.78**. The equal-weighted candidate and value-weighted market differ in size and sector exposure, so this comparison cannot isolate R&D as the cause. An independent Claude Opus review reproduced the arithmetic and examined the source mapping; the original builder was Codex Terra. A separate local arithmetic check was not retained as an artifact.

**Post-hoc loss and recovery check:** with the same monthly returns and 1% candidate cost assumption, the R&D portfolio's highest prior month-end value was in February 2021 and its deepest subsequent trough was March 2025, down 79.32%. It had not regained that February 2021 value by December 2025, **58 months later**; it was still 72.31% below it. On those same February 2021–March 2025 endpoints the broad market rose 45.10%. These are cumulative, pretax comparisons on identical dates, not independently selected stress episodes or proof of causes. The market is value-weighted while the R&D portfolio is equal-weighted and may contain different companies; turnover and individual stock holdings are unknown. This exploratory, hindsight-selected check was computed after the initial screen and cannot serve as a new pass/fail rule. The source portfolio mapping, prices and this calculation still await independent review.

**SPY context for that same selected episode:** the separately frozen SPY adjusted-close series rose **56.19%** from the February 2021 month-end to the March 2025 month-end and **92.06%** from that starting date to December 2025, with distributions reflected in adjusted close. The R&D portfolio fell 79.32% and 72.31% over those respective endpoints after its assumed cost; SPY has no explicit trading-cost or tax deduction. The SPY file SHA-256 is `3575a86f0d760a3ab46164ce43aefe792068caec5a265521160d7de998c24a44`; the three prices correspond to the NYSE's last trading dates of their months. This is a post-hoc, pretax comparison on a previously examined interval, not a prospective test, a simulated portfolio substitution or proof of an executable difference. The vendor's underlying prices and adjustments were not independently verified.

**Portfolio breadth:** the provider reports between 301 and 683 stocks per month across the 606-month test (median 414); during 2021–2025 it reports 393–522 (median 466). These are reported stock counts, not verified identities, independent holdings, sector diversification or investability. A small stock sleeve would not replicate this portfolio merely by selecting a few of its members; the impact of doing so is unknown.

**Post-hoc period check:** The independently reviewed 1%-cost annualized returns were 26.89% versus 14.39% in July 1975–December 1995, 24.50% versus 7.10% in 1996–2010, and **1.83% versus 13.83% in 2011–2025**. In the last ten years, 2016–2025, they were **−1.82% versus 14.72%**. The candidate's yearly returns in 2022–2025 were −48.42%, −12.39%, −8.41% and −7.30%, respectively. These periods were examined after results and are descriptive, not independent tests. The review also found a substantial small-company exposure; its additional factor regressions and alternative comparator were not independently reproduced here and do not change the fixed comparison.

**Action:** close this tested portfolio specification. Its high long-run average is concentrated in older decades, recent results are poor, and its drawdown is not consistent with demonstrated loss control. This does not establish that every related question fails, and it authorizes no paper or live trading. No prior verdict or freeze changes.
