# Proposed expected-investment-growth stock screen

**Proposal only; no historical return calculation has been approved or run.** This investigates whether a published large-stock portfolio sorted by forecast investment growth outperformed a broad market after an assumed cost. It is a separate question from earlier screens, not a reversal of their NO-GO results.

- **Candidate:** Global-q monthly value-weighted total-return portfolio with `rank_ME=2` and `rank_EG=3` (large stocks, highest forecast investment-growth group). Its expected-growth forecast uses valuation, cash flow and return-on-equity information; profitability overlap with earlier work remains unresolved.
- **Comparator:** the Global-q broad market, `R_MKT + R_F`, from the same July 2026 release. It is not SPY; a pass would not establish outperformance against SPY.
- **Windows:** January 1967–December 2025 for the full-history statistical test, and January 1994–December 2025 for the recent growth comparison. These reflect the provider's stated coverage, not an unseen holdout.
- **Proposed costs:** subtract 1% per year from candidate returns in equal monthly amounts, with a 2% stress reported for information only. These are assumptions, not measured execution costs; results are pretax.
- **Proposed hurdles:** both a 12-lag Newey–West t of at least 3 and one-sided p at most 0.00135 on full-history monthly excess returns after the 1% cost, and at least +1 percentage point per year of annualized compound growth after the 1% cost against the comparator in January 1994–December 2025. Show wealth and month-end maximum drawdown descriptively. Any missing or invalid month in 1967–2025 stops the run without a result; undefined full-window inference gives an unresolved overall result.
- **Multiple testing:** the t threshold is single-test, with no Holm correction across this and previous research screens. The approved Part A standard calls for Holm correction across a registered family; this proposal does not establish that its requirements are met or authorize later stages. The expected-growth idea and historical data are previously published and related to prior profitability and investment studies; a pass is not independent discovery.
