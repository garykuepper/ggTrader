# Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift (PEAD): NO-GO — Eval-Window-Dependent Result Overturned by Matched-Window Blend Test

**Classification:** Internal Quantitative Research & Engineering Strategy
**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Audience:** Principal Engineering Team & Quantitative Research Collaborators

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Engine Audit

This report tests `src/ggTrader/lab/strategies/pead.py` (`PeadStrategy`):
candidate #12 from `WEB_RESEARCH_CANDIDATES.md`. Mechanism: equal-weight
the quintile of the eligible universe with the *largest* recent positive
earnings surprise (underreaction drift), longing the winners rather than
avoiding a risky quintile — the opposite ranking direction from
`idio_vol`/`max_effect`/`short_interest`. Built on a new yfinance-backed
data pipeline (`src/ggTrader/lab/earnings_surprise_data.py`) — unlike the
analyst-revision-momentum candidate (#2, found infeasible the same day),
`Ticker.get_earnings_dates()` genuinely exposes deep, queryable historical
realized-surprise data (back to ~2002 for most symbols), not just a
rolling current-quarter snapshot. Backfilled 62,552 rows across 949 SP500 +
Russell 2000 symbols.

**This report documents a real methodological trap, not just a rejected
strategy.** An initial full-history test (`eval_start=2015-06-01`, 40
folds) showed a striking result: PEAD on SP500 beat SPY outright (OOS
Sharpe 0.93 vs 0.76-0.90, healthy WFE 1.01, gate pass 77.5%, regime halt
only 20% — far healthier diagnostics than every other candidate closed
this session) with a genuinely low 0.422 OOS-return correlation to the
deployed ensemble core. Per this project's own established playbook (the
`idio_vol` precedent), a promising standalone+diversification result gets
decided by an actual blend test, not left as a standalone number. **That
blend test overturned the finding.** Re-run on the exact window the
deployed 3-sleeve blend was validated on (`2021-01-31`–`2026-04-30`,
matched to `roadmap.md`'s July 13 entry), PEAD/SP500's edge evaporates
entirely — OOS Sharpe 0.58, tied with SPY's own 0.58 — and adding it as a
4th blend sleeve makes the deployed blend measurably worse. The initial
"beat SPY" result was concentrated in the pre-2021 portion of the longer
window and is not robust to the exact eval window that matters for a
deployment decision. This is the same eval-window-drift pitfall
`roadmap.md`'s July 13 entry already documented for the "1.12 baseline"
figure — caught here before it reached a live-config decision, not after.

The 3-sleeve baseline reproduced exactly (Sharpe 1.14, MaxDD -5.39%,
matching `roadmap.md`'s documented July 13 figures precisely — confirms
no methodology drift in this session's tooling). Adding `pead@sp500`
**reduces Sharpe by 0.08 and widens MaxDD by 1.12 points** — a clean
rejection, the same qualitative outcome as `idio_vol`'s July 8 4-sleeve
test (Sharpe 1.03→1.01 there).

**Diversification check** (initial long-window run,
`scripts/pead_correlation_check.py`): OOS return correlation to the
deployed core = 0.422, beta = 0.472 — moderate, comparable to `idio_vol`'s
0.447. This number is not wrong, but it's now understood to characterize a
return stream whose apparent edge (the reason the correlation looked
interesting in the first place) doesn't survive to the matched window —
low correlation to a core that isn't itself outperforming on that window
doesn't buy anything.

Russell 2000 was not re-tested on the matched window — the long-window
result there already tied SPY (not beat it) with a much higher regime-halt
rate (60% vs SP500's 20%), so a matched-window retest would not change the
qualitative conclusion and wasn't worth the additional compute.

## 4. Completed & Closed Research Arcs (Do NOT Re-Propose)

**A. PEAD, longing the top-surprise quintile, SP500 — REJECTED (matched
window).** OOS Sharpe 0.58, tied with SPY's own 0.58 on the exact window
the deployed blend was validated on. The long-window (2015-2026) result
that appeared to beat SPY (Sharpe 0.93) was an artifact of pre-2021
performance and does not replicate on the relevant recent window — treat
any future "PEAD beats SPY" claim with this specific caveat: check the eval
window before trusting the headline number.

**B. PEAD as a 4th blend sleeve (`pead@sp500` alongside the deployed
`ensemble@sp500,midcap400,nasdaq100`) — REJECTED.** Sharpe 1.14→1.06,
MaxDD -5.39%→-6.51%, a clean regression on both dimensions. Second
diversification-sleeve candidate rejected this way after `idio_vol`
(2026-07-08) — the pattern across both: a moderate (0.42-0.45) correlation
to the deployed core is necessary but not sufficient for a blend
improvement; the candidate's own standalone Sharpe also has to clear a real
bar, and neither did on final scrutiny.

**C. PEAD, Russell 2000 (lower-coverage universe) — REJECTED.** OOS Sharpe
0.77, tied with SPY, regime halt active 60% of folds (vs SP500's 20%) —
contrary to the literature's own expectation that PEAD should be *stronger*
in lower-coverage names (Martineau 2022's large-cap-decay finding implies
the opposite ranking). Likely explained by this lab's Russell 2000 universe
being a partial ~200-symbol subset (not the full ~2000), a known,
previously-documented data limitation (`roadmap.md`'s diversification
section) rather than evidence against the literature's premise.

## 5. Operational Roadmap: Recommended First Action

**Do not deploy `pead` in any configuration.** No live-config changes.

**Process note for future candidates:** this arc is a useful case study for
why this project's playbook insists on a matched-window blend test before
any standalone result is trusted, even a genuinely healthy-looking one
(WFE > 1.0, gate pass > 75%, low regime-halt). A long, favorable-looking
eval window can hide the fact that the edge is concentrated in a specific
historical stretch that doesn't overlap with the window that actually
matters for a deployment decision. Any future candidate that shows a
standalone "beats SPY" result should be re-verified on the deployed
blend's exact eval window before being reported as promising, not after.

## 6. Contrarian Evaluation & Parked Research

**Contrarian question:** is the pre-2021 outperformance itself informative
— e.g., did PEAD work during a specific regime (the 2016-2020 low-rate,
low-dispersion market) that could plausibly recur, making this a
regime-conditional sleeve rather than a dead strategy?

**Resolution:** Not worth pursuing without a much more specific regime
hypothesis than "it happened to work before 2021." Every fold winner in
the strong pre-2021 stretch was `quintile4` with a `max_age_days` value
that varied fold-to-fold (the same instability pattern seen elsewhere in
this project's history, e.g. Kelly sizing's 7/17 stability, that has
consistently indicated noise rather than a stable regime-dependent edge).
Building a regime classifier to gate PEAD on would be substantial new
engineering chasing a single historical stretch — not queued. Closing this
line.
